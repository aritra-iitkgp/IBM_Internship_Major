Understanding Customer Churn in Telecom

A data-driven approach to predicting customer retention

Posted as part of the IBM Telco Churn Analysis Project on August 05, 2026

Customer churn is one of the most important metrics in the telecommunications industry. It represents the percentage of customers who stop using a company’s services over a given period of time. High churn rates can significantly impact revenue and long-term growth.

In this project, we analyzed the IBM Telco Customer dataset to identify the key factors that influence whether a customer is likely to leave or stay. The dataset contains information about customer demographics, account details, and the services they have subscribed to.

Why Churn Prediction Matters

Acquiring a new customer is generally much more expensive than retaining an existing one. By predicting which customers are at risk of churning, companies can take proactive actions such as offering personalized discounts, improving service quality, or providing better support.

Through exploratory data analysis, we observed that factors such as contract type, tenure, monthly charges, and the presence of additional services (like tech support or online security) play a major role in customer retention.

Machine Learning Approach

Multiple machine learning models were trained to predict churn, including Random Forest, XGBoost, Logistic Regression, and Naive Bayes. Since the dataset was imbalanced, SMOTEENN was used to improve the model’s ability to correctly identify customers who are likely to churn.

After evaluating the models, the best-performing ones were saved and integrated into this web application. Users can now input customer details, select a model, and receive a real-time prediction.

Key Insights

Customers with month-to-month contracts and higher monthly charges tend to have a higher chance of churning. On the other hand, customers with longer contracts and longer tenure are more likely to stay with the company.

This project demonstrates how data analysis and machine learning can be combined to create a practical tool that supports better business decisions in the telecom sector.
